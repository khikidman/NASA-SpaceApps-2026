## Radio: communicate, then see the result

Connecting circuits and sending a test message connects to spacecraft exchanging
information by radio. NASA’s Deep Space Network uses large antennas to receive
spacecraft data and send instructions. The drawn waves, matching shapes and
instant test are authored representations, not a radio-engineering procedure or
a realistic signal-delay model. No waveform value is labeled in scientific units.

Source: [NASA Space Place: communicating with spacecraft](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/dsn-antennas/en/).

## Detector: an instrument can reveal something unseen

Apollo astronauts placed seismometers that revealed moonquakes. Placing, leveling
and recording a test trace gives the player a concrete setup → observation loop.
Our trace, flat-ground choice and foot settings are illustrative. They do not
reproduce Apollo equipment, report live data, or explain the story quake’s cause.

Source: [NASA: Moonquakes](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moonquakes/).

## Ice: look for evidence before collecting

Water ice has been confirmed in some permanently shadowed polar locations.
Scanning before collection encourages checking a clue instead of collecting any
rock. The cave, obvious blue crystals, visible seams, locations and scanner icons
are fictional teaching cues, not predictions of what a lunar explorer would find.
The lesson does not claim that every shadow contains ice or that ice occurs only
in shadows.

Source: [NASA: Moon Water and Ices](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-water-and-ices/), especially the 2018 confirmation section.

## Kitchen: connect equipment to daily life

Orion’s crew systems include a potable-water dispenser for rehydrating food and
drink packages and a food warmer. Fixing the kitchen gives a comfortable reason
to care about water and working equipment. Our gear/filter/tray machine and pipe
rotation are fictional, rather than a schematic of Orion’s food system.

Source: [NASA: Orion Crew Systems](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/crew-systems/), food and water section.

## Melter: processing needs checks

The space station’s water-recovery equipment treats recovered water in several
steps and checks its purity. Water that fails its checks is processed again.
Loading, warming and filtering therefore leads to a discussion of processing,
without claiming to simulate the real equipment. The three heat bands are
arbitrary game targets, not temperatures, lunar thermodynamics or a safe-water
recipe. Melting lunar ice and using one filter alone would not establish potability.

Source: [NASA: Environmental Control and Life Support Systems](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss/), Water Recovery System.

## Exercise: maintain the explorer, too

NASA describes aerobic and resistance exercise as helping maintain astronauts’
bones and muscles. The two routine choices make both kinds visible in the game.
The four movements, fictional hours, Energy score and skip outcome are gameplay
balance, not medical advice, exercise dosage or a measure of physiological health.

Source: [NASA: The Human Body in Space](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/the-human-body-in-space/), gravity section.
